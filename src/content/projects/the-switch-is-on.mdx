<Chapter eyebrow="01 · The problem" title="Good tools nobody could reach.">
  <Media slot="media" src="sio/old-homepage-2024.webp" alt="The 2024 Switch Is On homepage: a beach hero photo, then tool cards and appliance sections further down" fit="cover" focus="top" ratio="16/10" large />

About 40% of visitors came for rebates, but the Incentive Finder sat below the fold, behind dense copy and stock photos. And the site looked so much like a lead marketplace that visitors kept asking what it was selling.

<Expander summary="What we found">

**Most visitors were on a phone.** 52% of visitors use mobile, and iOS alone is 42%. A buried tool on a desktop is a tool nobody finds on a phone.

**Few searches ended in a conversation.** Only 7.7% of people who searched for a contractor went on to contact one.

**The nonprofit advantage was invisible.** SIO looked like Angi or Thumbtack. When the site tried to explain that it sold nothing, it made things worse: in testing, the more the site said what it wasn't, the more suspicious people became.

**Every new program meant rebuilding.** Three finders had each grown their own layout, filters, cards and icons. Any new partner or brand meant redoing decisions that had already been made somewhere else.

</Expander>
</Chapter>

<Chapter eyebrow="02 · The front door" title="One word, two paths.">
  <MediaGrid slot="media" min={300}>
    <Media src="sio/homepage-laptop.webp" alt="The Switch Is On homepage on a laptop: the headline, two buttons, Take me to incentives and Find me a pro, and a row of appliance cards below" fit="cover" ratio="4/3" size="half" />
    <Media src="sio/homepage-mobile.webp" alt="Three phone screens from the site: a heat pump article, the contractor sign-up banner above How can I go electric, and the We have so much info to share cards" fit="cover" ratio="4/3" size="half" />
  </MediaGrid>

"Switch" sounds like effort, "electrification" sounds technical, and "decarbonize" sounds political. We replaced all three with one benefit-led idea, *better*, and a homepage that asks one question: *Take me to incentives* or *Find me a pro*.

<Expander summary="How the homepage works">

**Stop asking people to give something up.** Modern electric appliances are better for your health, your comfort, your wallet and your future. Visitors finish the sentence with whatever motivates them, and they don't get a lecture.

<Cycle
  frames={['sio/better-cheaper.webp', 'sio/better-professional.webp', 'sio/better-efficient.webp', 'sio/better-easy.webp']}
  alt="Homepage section titled Better heating and cooling starts here, with four cards: Make it cheaper, Make it professional, Make it efficient and Make it easy, each taking a turn in its own color"
  ratio="200/129"
  size="half"
/>

**Show trust instead of claiming it.** We cut the defensive "we don't sell anything" copy. Trust now comes through how the site behaves: methodology notes on tool pages, a quiet strip of partner logos, and data sources linked at the bottom of each page.

**Five modules, one job each.** Below the two paths, each module can be measured and changed on its own. Contractors got their own entry point in the navigation and a Pros page in the same voice as the rest of the site.

The redesign also opened a new path: 680 to 1,128 people a month now click through to a rebate provider's site.

</Expander>

<Chapter eyebrow="03 · Where trust gets decided" title="Trust is won on the card, not the profile.">
  <Video
    slot="media"
    src="sio/badge-row"
    poster="sio/badge-row-poster.webp"
    label="Contractor cards in search results: tapping the stacked program badges opens them into a row with each program's name"
    tone="sio"
    ratio="850/640"

Viewing a contractor's full profile only moves contact from 4.8% to 6.8%. Homeowners decide at the card in the results list, so that's where I put the proof, and where every partner wanted their badge.

<Expander summary="Proof, badges and the other side of the lead">

**Use proof, not adjectives.** I wrote a framework that separates four kinds of signal that had been mixed together: contractor type, performance tier, program membership and manufacturer certification. The tier system is anchored to verified heat pump installs rather than lead volume. The first piece to ship was a "Verified Installs" counter, added to the existing card so it didn't have to wait for the full overhaul.

<Media src="sio/card-anatomy.webp" alt="Contractor card guidelines: a card with labeled rows for logo, title, reviews, location, details and action, each with its height and padding" tone="sio" size="half" />

**Close the loop on the contractor side.** A lead only matters if a contractor answers it. I designed the lead flows in the [Contractor Hub](/work/contractor-hub): receive, acknowledge, accept or decline. In the 60 days after launch, leads acknowledged by a contractor went from 18% to 61%, and 824 were acknowledged in the first month alone. Response speed now counts toward a contractor's ranking in search.

<Media src="sio/hub-response.webp" alt="Contractor Hub explainer titled Why does this matter: fast responses help you appear higher in search results, and declining a lead doesn't lower your score" tone="hub" size="half" />

**Every badge is a political decision.** Every partner program cared about whose badge showed and how prominently. My constraint was that badges never shrink. They got their own row above a pinned "Get in touch" button, and the results went from three columns to two so badges wouldn't wrap. I also raised an equity problem: ranking paid or partner badges first quietly pushes down the programs that don't pay.

</Expander>

<Chapter eyebrow="04 · The system underneath" title="The system had stalled twice. I changed who decides.">

Two design-system efforts had stalled before mine, with pull requests closed without merging. Nobody owned the yes. I became the single owner for review, pages moved over one at a time as they were needed, and a 12-point checklist became the definition of done.

<Expander summary="Inside the 13 role colors">

**Then engineering turned down my pipeline.** There were two token systems built in opposite directions: 160 primitives and 74 aliases in Figma, per-brand theme files in the repo, kept in sync by hand. I built a `tokens.json` pipeline and a custom Figma plugin to connect them. Engineering decided against a JSON layer between Figma and code, which made my merged plugin unnecessary.

Instead of defending it, I simplified the system to **13 role colors, named the same way in every brand**. The code theme files became the source of truth. With matching names in Figma and code, AI can compare them directly, and there's nothing left to map between the two.

<Media src="sio/role-colors.webp" alt="Table of the 13 role colors with their values for SIO, Colorado Contractor Hub, Power Ahead Colorado and WattSipper" tone="hub" />

**The proof is what got faster.** WattSipper, BDC's first white-label, launched as an Incentive Finder set up as a configuration rather than built from scratch. When the team redesigned the Incentive Finder's detail view in April 2026, they built it quickly on this system. The share of searchers who opened an incentive's details went from 18% to 44%, and on iOS from 16% to 47%. Detail views went from 4,257 to 5,692 a month.

<Chapter eyebrow="05 · What didn't move" title="The Hub made responses visible. Not every contractor responds.">

Fewer searches turn into leads than when we started, and a third of leads still go unanswered. The card is the next thing to fix.
