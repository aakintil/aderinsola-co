<Chapter eyebrow="01 · The problem" title="Great tools nobody could find.">
  <Media slot="media" src="sio/old-homepage-2024.webp" alt="The 2024 Switch Is On homepage: a beach hero photo, then tool cards and appliance sections further down" fit="cover" focus="top" ratio="16/10" large />

In 2024, only about 1 in 70 visitors ever reached the Incentive Finder. And the site looked so much like a paid lead marketplace that visitors kept asking what we were selling.

<Expander summary="What user testing showed">

Rebates were the top reason people came to the site, but the Incentive Finder sat below the fold, behind dense copy and stock imagery. The tool most visitors wanted was the hardest one to reach.

Contractors had the opposite problem. They landed on what looked like a homeowner site, assumed it was not for them and left, so they never found the free leads waiting for them.

Then there was trust. When the site explained that SIO was a nonprofit, testing showed it sounded defensive and made people more suspicious, not less. Saying it was not enough; the site had to show it.

</Expander>
</Chapter>

<Chapter eyebrow="02 · The reframe" title="One word changed the brief: better.">
  <Cycle
    slot="media"

The problem was language before it was layout. "Switch", "electrify" and "decarbonize" made it sound like work. Modern electric appliances are simply better: cheaper with local incentives, installed by verified pros, more efficient and easy to get.

<Expander summary="How we landed on better">

The mission words described what we wanted homeowners to do, not what they would get. "Better" turns that around: every message answers what is in it for you. The homepage carries it as four cards, one reason each, so a visitor sees the benefit before they see the ask.

</Expander>

What do you need? Two paths above the fold: find incentives or find a pro. Everything below them does one job and is measured on its own.

<Expander summary="Five modules, one job each">

- **Hero:** clear in three seconds.
- **Tool crossroads:** sends people to incentives or to contractors.
- **Benefit carousel:** carries the four "better" themes.
- **About SIO:** builds trust through behavior, not claims. This is where the nonprofit lesson from testing landed.
- **Contractor gateway:** gives pros a direct route to free leads, so they stop bouncing off a homeowner site.

<Chapter eyebrow="05 · The design system" title="The system had stalled twice. I changed how it got made.">
  <MediaGrid slot="media" min={220}>

Past attempts died in review, not in build. I became the single decision owner, moved pages over one at a time as they were touched, and made a shared checklist the definition of done.

<Expander summary="Inside the token pipeline">

I built a token pipeline and a custom Figma plugin to keep design and code in sync. Engineering then decided against adding a layer between Figma and code. Rather than defend the work, I collapsed the system to 13 role colors defined the same way for every brand, made the code the source of truth, and kept the plugin as a backup.

<Chapter eyebrow="06 · The finders" title="Trust is won on the card, not the profile.">
  <Media slot="media" src="sio/confirmed-installs.webp" alt="Contractor cards with verified licenses and confirmed installs" fit="cover" large />

Three finders had grown their own shells, filters and cards. The data showed homeowners decide on the card, so that is where I put the proof: verified licenses and confirmed installs.

<Expander summary="Three finders, one system">

The finders stayed separate products, each with its own job. What changed is that they now answer to one design system: the same shell, filters and card. The rule for bringing them in was conform first, then converge. Each finder moved onto the shared components before any of them changed how it worked.
